# The demo story — scope, decided

Decided 17 Sep 2026. Volkan writes it; Claude supplies a scaffold at most,
never the prose. It gates the video and the case study, and it is the only
launch item nobody else can do.

## The shape: a vertical slice

Chosen over "small and complete" and over "two tiny stories". The opening
**30–40 scenes** of something larger, ending on a deliberate cliff.

The industry term is the point: a vertical slice is a recognised portfolio
format, so it reads as a deliberate choice rather than as something
unfinished — PROVIDED two things are true, both of which are on Claude's
side to check and his side to decide.

## The two risks, and what defuses them

**1. "Vertical slice" vs "abandoned" lives entirely in the last screen and
in Check Story.** The ending must be a written ending — *End of Act One*, a
line that lands — not a scene that happens to have no choices. And before
anything is recorded, Check Story has to come back clean: no unreachable
scenes, no choices that lead nowhere, no gates that can never open. Three
dangling choices is what makes a slice look like someone who ran out of
steam; a clean report plus a deliberate ending makes the same 35 scenes look
like a chapter.

**2. A 35-scene graph shown flat is noise in a video.** This is the real
argument against the scope, and the answer is already built: chapters fold
to a single block. The map shot in the video is four folded chapter boxes
and the connections between them, then ONE unfolds to show the inside of an
act. That is a better demonstration of the Story Graph than a flat map ever
was — the feature doing the exact thing it was built for.

## What it means for the rest of the plan

- **It is the first real load test the app has had.** Every project the
  tests use is seeded and small. Thirty-plus scenes with real prose,
  entities, variables and conditions will find things the suite cannot.
  Expect bugs, and expect them to arrive while he is trying to write — which
  is the worst time, so save safety landing first is well timed.
- **It raises the value of Auto Layout round two.** Layout quality does not
  really show until the map is big; his own story at full size is the honest
  test case for it, and better than any synthetic graph Claude can build.
- **It raises the cost of losing work**, which is the same argument again
  for the atomic write and the `.bak`.
- **Scenes should be filed into chapter groups from the start**, not
  organised later — the folded-map video shot depends on it, and so does
  Auto Layout looking good.

## What Claude may and may not do

May: a structural scaffold if asked (scene titles, chapter groups, where the
branches meet), bug fixes found while he writes, and Check Story triage.

May not: write the prose. It is a narrative-design portfolio piece; the
writing is the thing being assessed.
